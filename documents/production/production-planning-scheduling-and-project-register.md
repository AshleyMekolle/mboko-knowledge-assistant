## Purpose

This policy defines how Mboko Media Group plans and schedules productions, prepares locations, manages safety and handles deadlines that are at risk. It also contains the project register, which is the authoritative record of every active production's status, team and dates.

## Scope

This policy applies to every documentary, series episode, interview shoot and branded production, whether filmed in Douala or elsewhere. It applies to employees and freelancers working on a Mboko production.

## Production Plan requirements

A Production Plan is required for every episode, branded film or shoot block. The Production Coordinator must submit it in Mboko Board **at least 15 working days before the first shoot day**. The Head of Production must approve it before any crew, location or equipment is booked.

Every Production Plan must include:
- Project code and episode number, for example MBK-MM-S02E05.
- Producer, director and crew list.
- Shoot dates, locations and travel plan.
- Contributors to be filmed, with confirmation from Editorial that research and contributor checks are complete.
- Equipment list, using asset tags from the Equipment Checkout and Return Policy (PROD-POL-002).
- Shoot budget, with confirmation from Finance and Operations that it fits the approved project budget in the Budget Planning and Control Policy (FIN-POL-002).
- Location risk assessment, described below.
- Planned post-production dates and the publication date agreed with Editorial and with Partnerships and Marketing.

## Scheduling and call sheets

- Approved shoot days are entered in the Mboko Board production calendar by the Production Coordinator.
- The Production Coordinator sends a **call sheet at least 48 hours before each shoot day** to every crew member and contributor. The call sheet lists call times, the location address and map link, the producer's work contact, the nearest medical facility and any safety notes.
- Shoot days for Production employees are protected. Production employees may not take leave on a scheduled shoot day unless the Head of Production approves it, as stated in the Leave and Attendance Policy (HR-POL-002).
- Shoots outside Douala require the Head of Production's approval of the travel plan and the use of equipment outside Douala.

## Locations and permissions

- Private locations require written permission from the owner or manager before the shoot. The permission is stored in Mboko Drive under `/Production/{ProjectCode}/Locations/`.
- Shoots in public places that need a permit from a local authority must have the permit confirmed at least 5 working days before the shoot day.
- If permission is withdrawn on the day, the producer stops filming at that location and informs the Head of Production.

## Location risk assessment

A location risk assessment is required for every shoot outside the Mboko office. The producer completes it in the Production Plan, and the Head of Production approves it.

The risk assessment must cover traffic and crowds, weather, electrical safety, night work, travel time and road conditions, security, and the welfare of contributors. Shoots at markets, at night or outside Douala must name a crew safety lead for the day.

## Incident reporting

An incident is any injury, near miss, equipment damage, theft, security threat or complaint from a contributor or member of the public during a production.

1. Make the scene safe and get medical help if anyone is hurt.
2. Inform the producer immediately.
3. For any injury requiring medical treatment, or any threat to safety, the producer informs the Head of Production and the Managing Director immediately by phone.
4. The producer submits an incident report in Mboko Board **within 24 hours** with the date, time, location, people involved, what happened and the action taken.
5. Equipment damage or loss is also reported under the Equipment Checkout and Return Policy (PROD-POL-002).
6. The Head of Production reviews every incident report within 5 working days and records the follow-up actions.

## When a production deadline is at risk

A deadline is at risk when any shoot, post-production, review or delivery date in the project register is likely to slip.

1. **Flag it.** The Production Coordinator or producer sets the item to "At Risk" in Mboko Board and informs the Head of Production **within 1 working day** of identifying the risk.
2. **Assess it.** The Head of Production agrees a recovery plan with the producer within 2 working days.
3. **Tell the affected teams.** If the publication date may change, the Head of Production informs the Editor-in-Chief and the Head of Partnerships and Marketing within 1 working day of the assessment.
4. **Client deliverables.** If a client or sponsor deliverable is affected, the Head of Partnerships and Marketing informs the client within 2 working days under the Sponsorship and Campaign Workflow (MKT-SOP-001).
5. **Escalate.** If a client deliverable will be delayed by more than 5 working days, the Managing Director decides how to proceed.
6. **Record.** The new dates are entered in Mboko Board and in this register.
